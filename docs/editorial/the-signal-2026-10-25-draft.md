## A smarter pause before holiday tech shopping
Week of October 25, 2026 — EDITORIAL DRAFT / NOT READY TO PUBLISH

### Reader copy begins
The holiday season has a way of turning technology shopping into a race. The quieter approach is to choose one useful thing, understand it, and let it earn its place in the household. This week's letter is less about what to buy and more about how to decide — a smarter pause before anything goes into a cart.

**One piece of news worth knowing.** Apple's iPhone Duo was announced on September 9 and became available in the United States on October 23. Those are two different moments worth keeping straight: the announcement is the introduction, the availability is when the product can actually be ordered and received. If you have been seeing conversation about it online, some of it may have started weeks before anyone could hold the device. This is news, not a buying recommendation — and the best reason to consider any new phone is that your current one no longer serves you well.

**Another update, on transparency.** On October 14, YouTube Music announced that it is rolling out labels for tracks created primarily with generative AI, with labels also expanding to main YouTube. Because this is described as a rollout, screens may differ from person to person for a while — some listeners will see the labels now, others later. The practical takeaway is familiar: when a song or video matters to you, check the source and description rather than assuming what you are hearing.

**A short checklist before any holiday tech purchase.** Four questions can prevent most regrets:

1. **Compatibility.** Will it work with the devices and services already in the home — the same account, the same operating system, the same voice assistant?
2. **Setup and support.** Who is going to install it and help when something goes wrong? A gift that arrives without a helper is often a gift that stays in a drawer.
3. **Return window.** Know the deadline before buying, not after. A generous return window is part of the value of the purchase.
4. **Privacy and settings.** Take five minutes after setup to review what the device shares, what it records, and who it can talk to.

None of this requires urgency. Most good tech decisions survive another day of thought.
